Portable Fight Forms

The question: when a fight built for a potential scene has to move on the fly, what form of write-up makes that a couple-of-edits job? Same fight below, three candidate forms. Pinned fights (keyed rooms, scenes that are the fight) skip all of this.

portable core — travels as-is scene binding — repaint on a move

Form 1 — today's shape, plus one line

The delivery is unchanged. A single If this fight moves line at the end names what must survive. Lightest possible fix; portability lives in the DM's head, prompted once.

Ambush at the Eelgrass Boathouse

Saltmere docks · 4 PCs, level 3 · Moderate · 600 XP (150 × 4)

Enemies — 600 / 600 XP

  • 4 × Cultist — 25 XP each → 100
  • 1 × Cult Fanatic — 450
  • 1 × Giant Poisonous Snake — 50

Terrain & setup

The boathouse loft holds two cultists with hand crossbows behind the winch. Stacked cargo crates give half cover across the main floor. The dock ramp is the only dry way in; the planking along the waterline is slick, and the snake hunts under it. The fanatic opens at the far waterline, mid-rite.

The spotlight — aimed: Wren (rogue)

The crate stacks form a climbable route to the loft that only Wren can take quietly — Cunning Action up the crates flanks the crossbows and turns the loft. Tell it plainly: the crates are described as stair-stepped toward the loft rail.

The complication — the drowning-rite (timer)

The fanatic is four rounds from finishing a rite over a bound hostage at the waterline. Each of the fanatic's turns advances it; disrupting the fanatic resets nothing but stops the clock while they fight. Round four, the hostage goes under.

Tactics

Round one the loft crossbows fire on whoever crosses the ramp; cultists screen the fanatic and give ground rather than chase. The fanatic never leaves the rite unless bloodied — then flees along the waterline. If the rite completes, everything left disengages into the water.

If this fight moves the one new line

Keep the four-round rite clock, a quiet climbable route for Wren, and enemies who plausibly serve the Eelgrass Cult near water — everything else is paint.

Form 2 — same shape, seam made visible

No restructuring. Every section is tinted by which side of the seam it sits on, tactics reference terrain by function, and the encounter-meta block is named as the portable core. A move = rewrite the clay sections.

Portable core travels as-is

> [!encounter-meta] > Enemies: 4x {monster:Cultist} (25) + {monster:Cult Fanatic} (450) > + {monster:Giant Poisonous Snake} (50) = 600 XP > Budget: Moderate, lvl 3, 4 PCs -> 150 x 4 = 600 XP (spent 600) > Objective: stop the drowning-rite before round 4 > Spotlight: aimed — Wren (rogue), quiet climb route to the high position > Habitat: Eelgrass Cult, near water

The block already carries everything scene-independent: roster, math, the complication's mechanic, the spotlight's function. New here: the Habitat line — where these enemies stay plausible.

Terrain & setup repaint

High position: the boathouse loft, reached by winch or crates — two cultist crossbows hold it. Cover field: stacked cargo crates, half cover, stair-stepped toward the loft rail. Choke: the dock ramp, the only dry approach. Hazard: slick waterline planking; the snake hunts beneath it. The fanatic opens at the far waterline, mid-rite.

The spotlight — aimed: Wren (rogue) travels

The cover field forms a quiet climbable route to the high position that only Wren can take — Cunning Action up it flanks the shooters and turns the fight's high ground. Tell it plainly.

The complication — the drowning-rite travels

Four rounds on the clock; each fanatic turn advances it; round four the hostage goes under. The rite happens past the hazard, so reaching it costs movement.

Tactics travels

Shooters on the high position fire on whoever crosses the choke; cultists screen the fanatic and give ground. The fanatic never leaves the rite unless bloodied, then flees through the hazard.

If this fight moves the move recipe

Repaint the terrain section only: find the new scene's high position, cover field, choke, and hazard. Check the Habitat line still holds. Everything in slate runs verbatim.

Form 3 — restructured: core, then binding

Two explicit parts. The core never mentions the boathouse; the binding is a role→noun table plus the opening. A move = refill one table. Heaviest change to the delivery format.

Form 3b — the floating fight: core only, no binding at all

A binding is written only when the fight is the scene — and a pinned fight just keeps today's full concrete shape. A floating fight ships as the core alone, and even its title goes scene-free. Nothing here names the boathouse; at the table you cast whatever scene fired into the four roles, out loud.

The Drowning-Rite

4 PCs, level 3 · Moderate · 600 XP (150 × 4)

Part 1 — the fight itself scene-free

> [!encounter-meta] > Enemies: 4x {monster:Cultist} (25) + {monster:Cult Fanatic} (450) > + {monster:Giant Poisonous Snake} (50) = 600 XP > Budget: Moderate, lvl 3, 4 PCs -> 150 x 4 = 600 XP (spent 600) > Objective: stop the drowning-rite before round 4 > Spotlight: aimed — Wren (rogue), quiet climb route to the high position > Habitat: Eelgrass Cult, near water

Needs four roles: a high position (shooters), a cover field forming a quiet climb to it (Wren's route), a choke (the approach), a hazard between the party and the rite.

Runs like this: shooters on the high position fire on the choke; cultists screen the fanatic, giving ground; the fanatic advances the rite every turn and never leaves it unless bloodied, then flees through the hazard. Round four, the hostage goes under.

There is no Part 2 by design

No binding ships with a floating fight. When a scene fires, reread the Needs line and cast that scene's nouns into the four roles aloud — the boathouse's loft, crates, ramp, and slick planks were only ever one possible casting. A fight that is a scene (or keys a dungeon room) doesn't use this form at all: it's written pinned and concrete, exactly like Form 1 without the note.

What a move costs at the table

Form 1Reread the whole block, hold the one-line note in your head, improvise the rest. Cheapest to write, dearest to run.
Form 2Rewrite the clay-tinted sections — in practice just Terrain & setup. Slate sections run verbatim because tactics already speak in roles.
Form 3Refill a five-row table. Cheapest move, but every fight now reads as two parts even when it never moves.
Form 3bNothing to move — the fight was never bound. Reread the Needs line, cast the live scene's nouns into the roles aloud. Pinned fights (the fight is the scene) stay fully concrete instead.
