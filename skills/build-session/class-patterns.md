# Class staging patterns

Staging patterns per 2024 class — the judgment layer rules text can't supply:
what to put in a scene so a class's statement fires, and the table psychology
of why it lands. **Patterns, not inventory** — the authoritative flagged list
for an actual PC lives on their player page (see the data ladder in
[`spotlight.md`](spotlight.md)); subclass and spell picks sharpen these entries, and a niche pick is
begging for a scene it can win. Verify any rules text you stage on via the
sourcing chain in [`rules-sourcing.md`](rules-sourcing.md) — several abilities
changed name or shape since 2014.

Aim at the *actual party*: the profile's player half (observed style)
outranks everything below.

## Barbarian

**Combat staging.** Easy to please: something to soak and something to cleave.
- Surround them with minions — minions exist to die, and carving through a
  crowd is the fantasy. Big single hits they can shrug off land almost as well
  ("I took 40 and I'm still standing").
- Hazards and AoEs let **Danger Sense** (advantage on DEX saves) quietly pay off.
- Reckless Attack loves enemies that punish nobody — and occasionally one that
  makes recklessness a real choice.

**Out of combat.** Strength as theater: gates to hoist, doors to break,
things to lift while the party files through underneath. **Primal Knowledge**
lets a raging Barbarian make Acrobatics, Intimidation, Perception, Stealth,
or Survival checks with Strength — invite the muscle-first solution instead
of routing every social beat through the face.

## Bard

**Combat staging.**
- **Countercharm** (level 7) is a Reaction that rerolls a failed save against
  Charmed or Frightened with advantage — it only exists if enemies frighten
  and charm. Fear auras and enchanters are Bard food.
- **Bardic Inspiration** now fires retroactively on a failed D20 Test: tense,
  visible failure moments are where the die gets to flip an outcome.
- Social and magical threats they can interrupt or finesse beat raw damage races.

**Out of combat.** Let them be the face — and let charm *work*. The classic
anti-pattern: every distraction, con, and minstrel gambit waved off as
"unnecessary" until the Bard never gets a cool moment. Illusions and charm
spells need a happy medium (Suggestion doesn't hand over the kingdom, but it
does get them past the gate). Talking their way out of a fight entirely is a
core Bard win — price it as a real victory, not a skipped encounter.

## Cleric

**Combat staging.**
- Put a horde of Undead in front of them at least once: **Turn Undead**
  (Frightened + Incapacitated, flees, breaks on damage) plus **Sear Undead**
  at level 5 turning failed saves into radiant burns. Trivializing that fight
  is the feature, not a problem.
- Check their subclass's **Channel Divinity** and stage its niche.
- With a healer in the party, don't pull punches — dropping a PC to 0 is what
  makes the clutch Healing Word / Revivify moment exist. Just don't
  manufacture downings *for* the Cleric at another player's expense.

**Out of combat.** Their god is a lens: undead desecration, faith politics,
divine omens. **Divine Intervention** (level 10) is now a guaranteed free
casting — give it stakes worthy of a miracle.

## Druid

**Combat staging.**
- Wild Shape is a creativity engine — when the plan is "I turn into a spider
  and web the dynamite under the bridge," stretch the stat block and get to
  yes unless it breaks the game.
- Terrain control (Spike Growth, Erupting Earth): have enemies actually
  blunder through it. Camouflaged Spike Growth is invisible to enemies who
  didn't see it cast — play that straight.
- **Wild Companion** / familiars: give the scout-shape jobs to do.

**Out of combat.** **Speak with Animals is always prepared** (2024 Druidic) —
keep a cast of squirrels, gulls, coyotes, and street trees in your pocket.
Animals and plants aren't tiny humans: "What are enemies?" Make the Druid
speak *tree*, bribe the mice, describe goblins to a shrub. They love it.

## Fighter

**Combat staging.** Read the build — the class is a chassis.
- Sword-and-board wants to guard someone; the archer wants a perch and sight
  lines; the Battle Master wants melee traffic for **Riposte** and a weapon
  worth **Disarming**; the Eldritch Knight wants to weave spell and steel.
- **Weapon Mastery** properties (Push, Topple, Vex…) are scene levers — a
  ledge behind the enemy makes Push a story.
- Everyone wants the action-hero round: **Action Surge** deserves a moment
  worth surging for, and **Second Wind**'s "I can do this all day" beat.

**Out of combat.** **Tactical Mind** (spend Second Wind on a failed ability
check) is new 2024 and easy to never notice — hand the Fighter hard checks
with real stakes so it fires.

## Monk

**Combat staging.** The poster child — almost everything needs setup.
- **Shoot them.** **Deflect Attacks** (level 3) reduces a hit and, on a
  reduction to 0, throws it back. No ranged enemies, no feature. Take it
  absurd when it earns it: the boulder, the ballista bolt.
- Verticality and distance: **Step of the Wind**, Unarmored Movement, and
  level 9 **Acrobatic Movement** (run up walls and across water) want
  rooftops, scaffolding, gaps, and chases. Let them pull a Matrix.
- **Stunning Strike** wants a target whose lost turn visibly matters — and
  shouldn't meet a legendary-resistance wall every single time.
- **Slow Fall** implies heights worth falling from.

**Out of combat.** Parkour as problem-solving; impossible acrobatic entrances;
the fastest character carrying the message no one else can deliver in time.

## Paladin

**Combat staging.**
- Give them something juicy to smite — a Fiend or Undead (extra Divine Smite
  die) that deserves the crit-smite memory. Note 2024 **Divine Smite** is a
  Bonus Action spell with a verbal component: a countering enemy caster makes
  it a duel, but don't lean on that often (see the anti-tell rules).
- **Aura of Protection** rewards tight formations — AoE-save fights let the
  Paladin *feel* the party surviving because of them.
- **Faithful Steed** is a free Find Steed: stage a battle where riding into
  it with the cavalry is an option. Let them mount up.

**Out of combat.** Oath as compass: temptations, judgment calls, protection
of the weak. **Lay on Hands** curing the Poisoned condition makes poison
plots personal.

## Ranger

**Combat staging.**
- The 2024 spine is **Hunter's Mark** (free casts via Favored Enemy): give
  them a target to *hunt* — a fleeing quarry, a specific mark among many.
- Terrain spells deserve victims — enemies trample through Spike Growth.
- A Beast Master's companion wants tandem-strike moments; the bond is the point.

**Out of combat.** Put exploration *on* them, by name: "You know ground like
this — what does the trail tell you?" Let them be Aragorn reading the
hobbits' tracks. Pass the tracking result as a note so the Ranger, not the
DM, narrates the find to the party. Foraging, navigation, weather-reading:
ask them first. Same animal/plant scenes as the Druid where their spells
support it.

## Rogue

**Combat staging.**
- **Sneak Attack every turn is the design target**, not an exploit — provide
  the flanking ally, the shadows to Hide in, the perch for **Steady Aim**.
  Work with it, never against it.
- **Cunning Strike** (2024) trades dice for Poison/Trip/Withdraw — enemies
  whose position or action economy matter make those trades delicious.
- The trap-to-disarm-mid-combat set piece: know your player — some Rogues
  love the side-objective, others resent missing a round of stabbing.

**Out of combat.** Skill checks are the Rogue's home turf. **Reliable
Talent** arrives at level 7 — "I can't roll below a 25" is the fantasy;
let the DC-30 lock and the impossible ceiling walk exist so only the Rogue
can beat them. Let them succeed where no one else can, and look cool doing it.

## Sorcerer

**Combat staging.**
- **Metamagic** is the identity: Subtle Spell wants a scene where casting
  visibly is dangerous (bound, watched, at the gala); Quickened wants
  action-starved rounds; Careful wants allies in the blast.
- **Innate Sorcery** is a power-stance — a round where +1 DC and advantage
  visibly flips an outcome sells it.
- A perfect **Counterspell** duel (2024: CON save, slot refunded on failure)
  against the enemy mage's showpiece spell — telegraph the big cast on purpose.

**Out of combat.** Their magic is *innate* — NPCs should react to what they
are (the scales, the storm-smell, the wild-magic reputation). Weird magical
phenomena resonate with their bloodline before anyone else notices.

## Warlock

**Combat staging.**
- Pact Magic recharges on Short Rests — an adventure rhythm with real short
  rests is itself a Warlock spotlight; **Magical Cunning** covers the
  desperate middle of a long day.
- Invocations are picks to honor: **Devil's Sight** demands darkness
  (including their own Darkness spell); **Repelling Blast** demands ledges,
  pits, and formations worth breaking.
- Counterspell duels fit them as well as Sorcerers.

**Out of combat.** The patron is a live NPC — **Contact Patron** (level 9,
free Contact Other Plane) is an explicit invitation to answer. Whispered
bargains, patron errands, the price of power: give the pact screen time.

## Wizard

**Combat staging.**
- Let the encounter-ender end an encounter: the Fireball that clears the
  goblins, the Hypnotic Pattern that freezes the room, Wall of Force
  splitting the fight. Balance across the *session*, not by nerfing the spell.
- The Counterspell duel is peak Wizard — bait it with the enemy caster's
  visible trump card.
- Prepared-list niche picks (divinations, Detect Thoughts, wall spells) are
  statements: build at least one scene a chosen spell can win outright.

**Out of combat.** They love feeling smart: lore to spout, magical mysteries
to be the detective on, arcane machinery only Arcana explains. **Ritual
Adept** and **Scholar** reward downtime-and-library scenes; **Memorize
Spell** (swap a prepared spell on a Short Rest) pays off when the next
problem is telegraphed — telegraph it. Familiars want jobs.
