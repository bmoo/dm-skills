# XP Budget — SRD 5.2 Encounter Math

The authoritative budget method from the System Reference Document 5.2
("Combat Encounter Difficulty"). This is the only sanctioned way to size a
fight in this campaign — never eyeball it from training-data CR math.

## The three steps

1. **Choose a difficulty.** Three bands:
   - **Low** — one or two scary moments; the party wins with no casualties,
     though someone may burn healing.
   - **Moderate** — without healing and resources it could go badly; a weaker
     character might drop, slim chance of a death.
   - **High** — could be lethal for one or more characters; survival needs
     smart tactics, quick thinking, and a little luck.
2. **Determine the XP budget.** Cross-reference party level with difficulty on
   the table below to get the **per-character** number, then **multiply by the
   number of characters** in the party.
3. **Spend the budget.** Every monster has an XP value in its stat block.
   Subtract each creature's XP as you add it. Spend as much as you can without
   going over — a few unspent XP left over is fine.

**Worked example (SRD).** A moderate encounter for **five level-3 characters**:
225 × 5 = **1,125 XP**. That buys, e.g., 2 Druids (450 each) + 9 Stirges
(25 each) = 1,125; or 1 Wight (700) + 1 Warhorse Skeleton (100) + 6 Skeletons
(50 each) = 1,100.

## Action-economy guardrails

The budget sizes *threat*, not the *shape* of the threat. The number of stat
blocks vs. the number of PCs is the action-economy lever — tune it
deliberately:

- **More creatures per side = more dice = more swing.** With more than **two
  creatures per character**, a lucky enemy streak can spike damage past what the
  budget implies — include **fragile creatures that can be defeated quickly** to
  bleed off that risk. This matters most at levels 1–2.
- **A lone boss against a full party is fragile the other way** — five PCs
  focus-fire and a single stat block can fall before it acts twice. If you spend
  most of the budget on one creature, give it a way to matter across rounds
  (legendary/lair actions, minions to soak attacks, or terrain that breaks line
  of sight) or accept it as a quick gut-punch, not the whole fight.
- **Cap the spike.** A creature whose **CR exceeds the party's level** can take
  out a character with a single action (an Ogre, CR 2, can one-shot a level-1
  Wizard). Use such creatures knowingly, and skip a monster whose signature
  feature lower-level PCs simply can't answer.
- **Cap at three monster types — hard rule.** Never put more than **three
  distinct stat blocks** in one encounter (multiple copies of a type are fine;
  it's the number of *kinds* that's capped). The best fights often pair one
  creature type with a complement (a wight leading its skeletons); two is usually
  plenty, three is the ceiling. More than three is unrunnable at the table.
- **CR 0 sparingly.** Worth-0-XP critters add bodies but no budget; use a swarm
  stat block instead of a pile of them.

## Adjusting on the fly

Die rolls and absences shift the real difficulty. A missing player warrants
**removing creatures**. Mid-fight, dial difficulty by having creatures **flee**
(easier) or bringing **reinforcements** (harder) — both read as in-fiction
events, not fudging.
