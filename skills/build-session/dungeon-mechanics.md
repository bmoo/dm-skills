# Dungeon-Wide Mechanics

Every dungeon carries **exactly one** dungeon-wide mechanic by default — the
DM may waive it ("vanilla") but never gets two: competing gimmicks blur both.
It is what makes this site play unlike any other, and it must be a *mechanic*,
not a mood — if it never changes a roll, a resource, or a decision, it's set
dressing, not a mechanic.

This is distinct from per-fight **complications** (those come from
the fight procedure's `complications.md`, one per fight): the mechanic belongs to
the *site* and is running whether or not initiative has been rolled.

## The rules-expression template

Every candidate on the slate is written in this shape, and the delivered
mechanic ships as this box:

- **Trigger/Clock** — what sets it off or advances it (time, noise, alarms,
  party actions, a schedule).
- **Effect** — what it does in game terms: DCs, damage, conditions, blocked
  or opened routes, altered rules.
- **Tells** — how the players *learn* it exists and how it works (the DMG's
  hint discipline: subtle or obvious, but present).
- **Exploit** — how mastering it pays. This clause is what makes the
  mechanic a toy rather than a tax: if the players can't ever turn it to
  their advantage, redesign it.

## Families

Grounded examples — translate to the anchor's fiction, don't copy.

- **Cycle / clock.** The site changes state on a schedule: a generator that
  powers doors and lights in phases, a patrol rotation, trains that make a
  tunnel lethal on a timetable, a foundry that vents scalding steam on the hour.
- **Escalating response.** The site notices intrusion and ratchets: security
  tiers that escalate per tripped alarm, a hive that grows more agitated, a
  warden who commits reinforcements in stages. (Pairs naturally with the
  active-roster notes in Step 5 — this family *is* the roster, systematized.)
- **Environmental modifier.** Zones where the rules run differently: warded
  rooms that suppress or amplify magic, mundane-decay hotspots where
  technology dies, consecrated or poisoned ground, deep cold or bad air as a
  meter the site presses on.
- **Moving element.** One thing that roams and rearranges the topology: a
  creature too big to fight that must be tracked and dodged, the one working
  elevator, walls or containers that shift on the clock.
- **Social overlay.** A standing arrangement that binds everyone inside:
  a truce between resident factions, guest-right enforced by something old
  and strong, toll-keepers on the good routes, a debt-ledger the inhabitants
  honor. (In a favor-economy campaign this family is home turf.)

## Pairing with the signature technique

When drafting the slate, flag pairings that reinforce: a cycle mechanic makes
a **divided level** breathe (the middle level unlocks only in the generator's
off-phase); an
escalating response makes **loops** matter (the long way around is the quiet
way); a moving element gives **multiple level connections** teeth (the
elevator is safe only when the roamer is elsewhere); an environmental
modifier can *be* the reason for **non-Euclidean** or **extradimensional**
space. A mechanic that never touches the structure is a missed pairing —
prefer candidates that make the chosen topology matter at the table.
