# Encounter design — skill guidance

These are this skill’s encounter-design defaults, not rules attributed to
the SRD. Campaign preferences and explicit caller instructions take precedence.
Use the XP ceiling from `xp-budget.md` alongside this guidance.

Creature count sets independent turns; distinct stat blocks set DM lookup
load. Tune both deliberately.

- **Set a turn target before picking CRs.** Read the campaign’s combat-tempo
  preference or the caller’s target. Without one, aim near one independently
  acting enemy per PC for an ordinary fight; this is a pacing default, not an
  SRD difficulty rule. State the intended count, then buy stronger creatures
  within the XP ceiling before adding bodies. Leave a remainder rather than
  padding the roster. A swarm or solo is a deliberate exception with a stated
  tempo tradeoff.
- **Count actual turns.** Multiattack is several attacks on one turn; it does
  not supply independent actors against control. Shared initiative still means
  multiple creature turns. Count active allies and summons separately in the
  total round workload; list legendary/lair actions and reactions separately,
  with their triggers and dependencies. Waves must state starting, peak-active,
  and total enemy counts; a later wave does not repair opening-round parity.
- **Check control resilience.** State what pressure remains if one enemy loses
  its turn and if one area effect catches the starting formation. Use dispersed
  positions, complementary reach/mobility, and sourced saves or traits where
  they fit the fiction. Higher CR alone is no protection, and extra attacks
  disappear with their actor. Preserve successful control’s payoff; do not
  improvise immunities or unbudgeted replacements to erase it.
- **Price every participant.** A hostile optional NPC spends enemy XP and adds
  a turn. An earned ally adds a friendly turn and changes practical difficulty;
  report that advantage separately rather than subtracting its XP from enemies.
  Publish an explicit roster and recomputed budget/turn count for expected
  absences, preserving the campaign’s target where feasible.
