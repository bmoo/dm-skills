# Encounter design — skill guidance

These are this skill’s encounter-design defaults, not rules attributed to
the SRD. Campaign preferences and explicit caller instructions take precedence.
Use the XP ceiling from `xp-budget.md` alongside this guidance.

Aim for a manageable round with meaningful choices and opportunities for the
party’s abilities to shine. Creature count sets independent turns; distinct
stat blocks set DM lookup load. Consider both when choosing the roster.

- **Choose the encounter’s shape before filling the budget.** Read the
  campaign’s combat-tempo preference or the caller’s target. Around one enemy
  per PC is a useful starting point for an ordinary mixed fight, not a universal
  minimum or maximum. Mobs, solos, and waves are valid encounter shapes; choose
  the shape that serves the situation and intended experience. When a roster
  would create too many turns for that purpose, buy stronger creatures within
  the XP ceiling or leave XP unspent. Extra bodies should contribute to the
  fight rather than merely consume the remainder.
- **Count actual turns.** Multiattack is several attacks on one turn; it does
  not supply independent actors against control. Shared initiative still means
  multiple creature turns. Count active allies and summons separately in the
  total round workload; list legendary/lair actions and reactions separately,
  with their triggers and dependencies. For waves, state starting, peak-active,
  and total enemy counts so their pacing is visible.
- **Make room for control to matter.** Consider what happens if one enemy loses
  its turn or an area effect catches a group, including the opportunity that
  creates for the party. Mobs can reward area control and clearing many enemies;
  a decisive control play or quick victory can be the intended payoff. When
  sustained pressure serves the encounter, use positioning, complementary
  roles, or sourced traits that fit the fiction. Evaluate that alongside the
  players’ opportunities, rather than requiring every fight to resist control.
  Honor successful control; do not invent immunities or replacement enemies
  solely to cancel its effect.
- **Account for participants and absences.** A hostile optional NPC spends
  enemy XP and adds a turn. An earned ally adds a friendly turn and changes
  practical difficulty; report that advantage separately rather than
  subtracting its XP from enemies. Publish an explicit roster and recomputed
  budget/turn count for expected absences, preserving the encounter’s intended
  experience and the campaign’s stated preferences.
