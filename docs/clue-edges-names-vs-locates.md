# Clue edges — names versus locates

The ruling and the evidence behind the *Lead typing — names vs locates* slot in
[the campaign contract](campaign-contract.md). It is a **record-model** question,
settled ahead of its consumer:
node-map's counterfactual mode
answers *"strike this clue — what still reaches?"*, and without the distinction
its orphan set is wrong.


**What is settled, in one place:**

- **Names-vs-locates is the right cut, and it is binary.** Graded on the frozen
  webs, not on plausibility. Three shapes that looked like a third value all
  collapse into *not a route*.
- **It lives in the clue-web entry, one type per edge** — the granularity the
  record already files at — discovered through the contract's *Lead typing* slot.
- **The degrade reads the type off the entry's own sentence and states the
  reading.** Never silence, never refusal. Where a sentence does not say, the
  edge does not count as locating.
- **Nobody writes it: `seed-clues` is unchanged.** The distinction is a passive
  read of what the DM already wrote.
- **Retrofit cost is zero required**, and bounded by a marker the record already
  carries where a campaign wants it pinned.

## The distinction

A clue-web entry is one **edge**: a source node, a target, and the sentence that
carries it. Two kinds:

- **locates** — following it puts the party in front of the target.
- **names** — it establishes the target, or a reason to go, and carries no way
  to get there.

As-built the conflation is free: there is an edge either way and the map draws
it. As-if it is the whole answer — strike the one edge that locates and the node
is orphaned while three edges still name it.

The type belongs to the **edge, not the clue**. Rosalyn Briarwood's one lead in
the Lost Vein scaffold is a locating edge into Emberwillow and a naming edge into
Redfang Castle. The record already files one entry per edge and mirrors it at
both ends, so per-edge is the granularity it has.

## Graded against the frozen scaffolds

Read out of the four frozen campaign scaffolds at `~/dev/ablation-scaffolds/`,
treated as read-only: **70 lead entries across 44 node pages with a clue-web
section** — 31 entries on 18 pages in the Lost Vein scaffold, 11 on 9 in
Gloamfen, 11 on 8 in Before the Storm, 17 on 9 in Wild Sheep Chase. Nothing here
argues from plausibility; every quotation below is in one of those trees.

**Where the distinction bites is narrow, and the record already marks it.** For
a target that is fixed and publicly sited — a city museum, a university, an inn,
a road — every inbound edge locates trivially and the two types coincide. The
distinction comes apart only on a target whose whereabouts are themselves in
play, and those pages carry a marker in their own frontmatter: `unlocated` (Wave
Echo Cave), `missing` (Halgrim Deepdelver, Finethir Shinebright), or `mobile`
(Sablewing, Pierre Kalison, Edgard Brandle, Dr. Isolde Fenwick, Guz). Redfang
Castle carries none but is marked twice in prose — "location unknown" in the node
index, and a callout on its own page: "The party do not know where it is."

**Nine of the 44 pages, and 20 of the 70 entries point at one of them.** Nobody
designed that marker for this; it is the record noticing the same class for its
own reasons.

The marker **over-selects, and bounds the work from above rather than naming the
biting class exactly**: Guz keeps a fortnightly market round and Edgard Brandle
is wherever his crew is, so both are `mobile` and both are trivially locatable.
That does not move the number, because the count is over inbound *edges* and not
over pages — Before the Storm carries three `mobile` pages and contributes one
entry.

### The Lost Vein finding, reproduced from the record

**Redfang Castle carries five distinct inbound leads and none of them locates
it.** In the order the record files them:

1. **Corvin Ashenbrook's commission** — the party are to "find Redfang Castle,
   search for Halgrim Deepdelver, rescue the dwarf, and retrieve his map." The
   live layer says why this is a naming edge and not a locating one: "He cannot
   say where the castle is; he has never seen it." **Names.**
2. **Rosalyn Briarwood's lead to Fernwyn** — the druid at Emberwillow "might know
   the whereabouts of Redfang Castle", or Stonesong Cave, "or both." A
   *prospective* locator: it carries the name of someone who may know, not the
   place. **Names.**
3. **The Redfang Hideout prisoners** — they "named King Grol and named his
   castle; they did not say where it is, and the party did not get it out of
   them." **Names**, in the record's own words.
4. **The Redfang tribe** — "Every band answers, in the end, to King Grol — and
   Grol holds a castle." **Names.**
5. **Halgrim Deepdelver** — the band that took him "answered to King Grol", and
   the prisoners "said their captives went up the chain, not into the cave."
   **Names.**

The node page reaches the same total on its own: "A name, a ruler, and nothing
else that is any use for finding it."

**Stonesong Cave carries two, and neither locates it either:** Halgrim "had the
only map anyone has heard of, and it went with him" — which locates the *locator*,
inside Redfang Castle — and the same prospective Fernwyn lead.

**The strike.** Strike the one lead Rosalyn gives. The record mirrors it in five
places, so a strike follows the mirrors, not the entries.

- Redfang Castle keeps four inbound edges and loses the only one that could ever
  have become a route. **Orphaned.**
- Stonesong Cave keeps one inbound edge whose payload sits inside Redfang Castle,
  which is now unreachable. **Orphaned.**

One strike, both destination nodes — the finding the parent spec records. Untyped,
the same strike leaves four edges standing at one node and one at the other and
reports **no orphan at all**.

### Not a Lost Vein artifact

- **Gloamfen.** Dr. Isolde Fenwick has two inbound leads. The university's is
  "a matter of public record, posted at the faculty gate" — it establishes her
  and says nothing about where she is. The Gilded Folio's is "anyone looking for
  her is told to try the Quill first." Only the second locates; strike it and she
  is orphaned with an edge still standing. The museum's lead to Orla Selke does
  the same thing in one word — the Vault's dossier "names its curator and flags
  her" — while Selke's own page locates her: "She is resident there; reaching her
  means going to the museum."
- **Before the Storm.** Sablewing's only inbound lead is Rubert Forrester, who
  "knows the dragon's reputation better than anyone on this coast" — naming. The
  dragon is approached through Adarath's abandoned lair, "the only place the party
  have confirmed it ever was."
- **Wild Sheep Chase — the counter-case.** The Oakhouse carries four inbound
  leads and at least three locate: the College's inquiry gives the address ("The
  address is the Oakhouse"), the Kingsoak names the turning ("The track to the
  Oakhouse leaves the main road a few miles out of town"), and Guz's buying goes
  "up the Kingsoak track." Typing changes nothing here. That is the point —
  typing costs nothing where the web is robust and is the entire answer where it
  is not.

### Two values, not more

Three shapes in the corpus look like they want a third value, and all three
collapse:

- **prospective** — "may know where the castle stands" (Lost Vein, twice).
- **stale** — "the only place the party have confirmed it ever was" (Before the
  Storm).
- **out of reach** — "He had the map. He was taken with it." (Lost Vein).

Each answers the counterfactual's only question the same way: following this edge
does not put the party at the target. *Why* it fails is prose the node page
already carries, and no consumer reads it. A third value would be decoration.
**The cut is binary.**

## The degrade, for a record that has never carried it

A campaign whose web has never marked the distinction still gets an answer. The
README's promise is "no required directories, page types, or campaign-record
structures", so the reader never refuses and never assumes silently:

1. **Type each edge from the entry's own sentence** — which is what the record
   already carries, in all four scaffolds, written before the concept existed.
2. **State the reading with the answer** — which edges were read as locating, and
   on what words. A stated assumption, never silence.
3. **Where a sentence does not say, the edge does not count as locating**, and
   the reading says so. The mode over-reports orphans rather than under-reporting
   them, because the question asked is *where is the single point of failure* and
   the error that makes the DM look is the safe one.

**Why not the candidate reading.** The parent spec floats *"treat every clue as
locating and over-report orphans"* as the candidate for this degrade and says
plainly that it is a candidate and not a ruling. Graded on the Lost Vein web it
fails: treating every edge as locating is arithmetically identical to the
as-built map, so the Rosalyn strike leaves four inbound edges at Redfang Castle
and one at Stonesong Cave and reports nothing — the finding the distinction
exists to preserve disappears, and the degrade under-reports where it was meant
to over-report. The **posture** the spec states is kept exactly: over-report, say
so, never silence or refusal. Only the mechanism that delivers it changes.

## Who writes it

**Nobody. It is a passive read of what the DM already wrote — `seed-clues` is
unchanged**, and no inventory-first chain is triggered by this decision.

All 70 entries predate the concept, across four situations, and on every target
where the distinction bites the sentence already states it. Typing at mint time
would add a required tag to every lead in every campaign to record what the
sentence carries anyway, and would still leave every pre-existing lead untyped —
it does not avoid the retrofit, it moves it. `seed-clues` does type exactly one
lead per run today, the delivery-timing tag on the forward/exit lead, and that
tag exists precisely because nothing in a lead's sentence carries when it should
land. This is the opposite case.

## Retrofit cost

**Zero required.** A record that has never carried the distinction reads to the
same answer through the degrade above, because the wording it already uses is
what the degrade reads.

A campaign that wants the distinction **pinned rather than inferred** states the
convention in its method handbook — clue-web layout is campaign-owned — and the
contract's *Lead typing — names vs locates* slot is where a skill discovers that
it did. The work is bounded from above by a marker the record already carries:
no edge outside an `unlocated` / `missing` / `mobile` target needs a mark, and
some inside one will not either. In the corpus above that ceiling is **20 of 70
entries, at 9 of 44 pages**. Everything else is trivially locating and earns
nothing from being typed.

## Not settled here

- **The grammar.** The struck-edge glyph, the orphaned-node box, and how the type
  is *drawn* are `node-map`'s visual vocabulary, owned by
  the counterfactual-mode design work
  along with their inventory rows. No inventory row lands with this ruling:
  nothing in shipped skill text changes, so no new checkable promise enters.
- **One observation, offered and not ruled.** `seed-clues` requires three
  independent leads at any place the players must go, and counts entries. Redfang
  Castle carries five, from five source nodes, and the record states plainly that
  the party cannot reach it. Whether that floor should count routes rather than
  entries is a doctrine question for the owner, not a consequence of this ruling.
