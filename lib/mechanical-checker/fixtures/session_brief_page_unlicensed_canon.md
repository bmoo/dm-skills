## Adventure Background

Eggs of this kind lie dormant for generations in cold ground, and cutting one
out of the peat accelerates what the cold was holding back. What comes out of
one is ravenous from its first breath and grows on what it is fed. Fenwick has
built a crystal box that will hold the thing quiet — the casing is done, the
seal is not, so the box cannot go on the job with them.

Fenwick ground the panes of that box herself over nine months in a rented room
above the Gilded Folio. The box is Fenwick's second attempt, the first having
cracked at the seal after five days under load. Orla Selke has been fencing
objects out of the Grayharbour Museum for forty years, since long before the
board made her curator. The Gloamfen crew pulled seven other unclassifiable
finds out of the peat that same season and sold every one of them through the
annex.

Fenwick identified the object by reading the party's own dig log, which is how
she knew to come to them rather than to the university. Samira Romero was hired
out of a mercenary company and answers to the museum's board rather than to
Selke. The Gemstone Wing was built as a vault, and its inner doors are the
museum's original ironwork.

A weighted net hangs above the inside of the staff entrance and drops on the
first person through it after closing — DC 14 Dexterity save or restrained until
someone cuts it away.

**Features.** Interior doors are locked after closing — DC 12 with thieves'
tools. Secret doors are DC 12 to spot. Ceilings run 30 feet through the public
halls and low through the service ways. Twelve guards hold fixed stations, and a
raised alarm draws 1d4 more guards per round from the nearest station. Alarm
spells sit on the marked interior thresholds and a staff pass card bypasses
them; while the museum is open to the public the alarms are unarmed, which is
why the gala is a way in.

### T1

Museum Square and the front steps. Samira Romero takes up the east portico after
eight and stays there.

### T5

The records room, off the service way. Selke's office door is locked and holds
her private correspondence, which is dull.

- **Show:** two entries for the same acquisition, one written over the other,
  and the purchase paperwork tucked in behind the gift record.
- **They learn:** the museum's own books were written to make this acquisition
  look like a gift. This is the third independent piece of evidence the party
  hold that unprovenanced finds are being papered through legitimate
  institutions.
- **Points at** *(behind the screen):* the papering revelation, which this
  completes. It does not name who wrote the false entry.

### T7

The Gemstone Wing. The egg begins to pulse at half past ten; anyone within reach
of the pedestal makes a DC 10 Wisdom save or feels it answer.

- **Show:** the thing on the pedestal is warm through the case glass, and it
  moves.
- **They learn:** the object is not an artifact and never was. It is alive, it
  is an egg, and it is very close to opening.
- **Points at** *(behind the screen):* the revelation that the Gloamfen produced
  something that is not an artifact — this lands it outright, because the party
  are holding it.

### T9

The Unearthed Café. Chairs on tables, a cold range, and a ladder in the store
room going up to the attic.

- **Show:** the movement order, on museum stationery, pressed with a private
  seal rather than the museum's.
- **They learn:** the curator ordered the wing's centre piece taken out of the
  museum tonight, using people who do not work here.
- **Points at** *(behind the screen):* the curator and the arrangement that pays
  a crew like this one.

### T10

The attic and the skylight. Cold, and open to the roofline.

### T11

The basement loading dock. A second sealed crate sits against the wall,
unclassified and unentered.

## Conclusion

The night resolves on one question — is the egg contained by midnight — and it
resolves either way.

- **Lead →** the second sealed crate in the basement points back at the Gloamfen
  and the site that produced both — same crew mark, same week, never opened.
- **Lead →** Fenwick's account points at the university: she was dismissed for
  something she did not do, and a review is a thing that can be forced.
